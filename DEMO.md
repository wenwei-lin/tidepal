# Demo video plan

About 90 seconds, English, recorded on the real desktop.

## Setup

- **Record the real screen**, not a fake one: Pip floating in the bottom-right corner over your code editor. It looks honest, and judges check that the SDK really runs.
- **Tool**: OBS (free). Source "macOS Screen Capture" for the display, plus your microphone and the app audio, so both your voice and Pip's voice are on separate tracks. 1080p or higher, 30 fps.
- **Sound**: wear no headphones only if the room is quiet; Pip ignores the mic while it speaks, so its voice won't be heard as you.
- **Light**: face a window or lamp; sit back far enough that your shoulders are in Pip's mirror.
- Record each shot as its own short clip; cut later.
- **Recording mode**: press **R** in Pip's window (or the ⏺ button). Pip gets bigger, captions get bigger ("You: …" under the pool, Pip's words in the bubble), and the controls hide until you hover the bottom edge. Press R again to go back.
- **What's on screen**: anything real. Pip doesn't read the screen — it reads *you*. Reading a Dev.to post is a good scene: you react to the post, Pip reacts to you.

## Editing

- Zoom (crop) into the Pip window for the reaction shots; keep a wide shot first so people see it lives on a real desktop.
- Add English subtitles for both your lines and Pip's.
- Keep the camera mirror visible in reaction shots: it shows the reaction matches your face.

## The idea to say out loud (for the post and the end card)

Most desktop agents only work *for* you and only know what you type. Pip is the other half: it notices *you* — your face, your breathing, your heartbeat — and gives feedback, so a desktop companion feels alive. Later the same signals could be handed to other agents (a coding agent that slows down when you look frustrated), but this demo is just the feedback.
